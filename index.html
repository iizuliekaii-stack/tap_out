Lyka's Cognitive Load Protocol

A two-phase system for high-focus study/work sessions, built around how she's actually wired: fast associative thinking that runs hot and burns out around 30 minutes, high extraversion and openness that need active refueling rather than passive rest, and an achievement-driven motivation system that likes visible progress.

Phase 1: Disengagement

Always first. Non-negotiable.

When: Immediately after a focus stretch ends — even if she feels like she could push through the fun/curiosity part first. Discharge before refuel, not the other way around.

Duration: 5 minutes

What it treats: Mental/cognitive fatigue specifically — nervous system activation from dense associative thinking.

This is not for physical fatigue, which needs a different response entirely: rest, food, hydration, sleep, etc. Physical-fatigue management is not covered in this protocol.

Protocol

  • Head down, eyes closed.
  • Noise-cancelling headphones on or complete silence.
  • No screens.
  • No input of any kind.
  • Let the system come down — no problem-solving and no planning the next task.

Skipping this phase and jumping straight to a "fun" recharge activity risks stacking new stimulation on top of an already activated state, making the reset less effective.

Phase 2: Recharge Menu

Purpose: Refuel motivation and curiosity — a different job than Phase 1.

This phase works with her traits — high openness, high extraversion, and achievement-striving — instead of asking her to sit in prolonged blank rest, which does not suit her particularly well.

Principle: Different flavor, same fuel. Stay in input/low-stakes mode — nothing that creates its own major cognitive load or performance pressure.

5-Minute Tier — Quick Top-Off

Type Example
Curiosity snack Short video clip on a topic she likes — physics, human geography, history, or another interesting subject.
Language rep A couple of Duolingo-style reps, conversation/ dialogue challenges, or a short foreign-language clip.
Social ping Voice note or quick text to a friend — banter, meme, or simple check-in.
Micro win Glance back at flashcards she already knows cold for a quick reminder of, "I've got this."
Sensory reset Make tea or coffee, step outside for air, or change the room's lighting or music.

15-Minute Tier — Moderate Refuel

Type Example
Curiosity rabbit hole One Wikipedia or YouTube spiral on something unrelated to the current work — a physics oddity, human geography fact, or a place she wants to visit.
Language immersion A full short video or podcast segment in Spanish, or another target language, on a topic she actually enjoys.
Real conversation A short call or voice chat with a friend — actual back-and-forth, rather than only texting.
Light movement Short walk, stretching, or a few easy climbing movements if available — not a workout, just enough movement to shift state.
Creative doodle Quick sketch, playing a song, messing with a photo, or another expressive activity with no stakes attached.

30-Minute Tier — Full Reset

Use when she's genuinely depleted, not as the default break.

Type Example
Deep curiosity dive Full documentary segment or long-form video on a subject she loves.
Social time Actual hangout or longer call with a friend.
Movement session Real climbing, a run, or a workout — combining physical recharge with a mental reset.
Language + connection combo Watch content in Spanish and then discuss it with someone afterward — combining curiosity, language learning, and social engagement.

Notes on Use

  • Start small, build slow. The goal over time is to stretch the roughly 30-minute focus window toward 60 minutes, but this should happen through gradual exposure: 35 → 40 → 45 minutes and so on, rather than forcing large jumps. Rigid jumps are more likely to backfire.
  • Fewer breaks, not smaller breaks, is the long-term win. As her cognitive stamina increases, she will likely need the recharge menu less frequently. The goal is not necessarily to make breaks shorter; it is to require fewer resets during the same amount of work.
  • Log the stretches. Because she responds strongly to visible progress — climbing sends, running times, grades, and similar markers — tracking statements such as "today I completed 35 minutes before needing a reset" turns cognitive stamina into another visible progress narrative.
  • Watch for the self-efficacy trap. If a stretch goal is placed too far ahead and missed repeatedly, it may reinforce the narrative of "I'm not good at this" rather than "I'm building this ability." Keep goalposts close, visible, and clearly incremental.
